01  /  Why

Real-robot evaluation is the bottleneck.

Ranking a handful of policies takes up to 360 rollouts per institution, run by hand over two weeks on physical hardware. It does not scale with the pace of new foundation models.

The bet: we don't need simulation to look real. We need it to order policies the same way real evals do.

The thesis

Validity is rank
preservation,

not photorealism and not dataset scale. If the sim leaderboard matches the real leaderboard, you can iterate in sim and trust the result.

Define the task
One YAML per task: a language instruction plus an ordered, cumulative milestone ladder.
04
Run the same policies in both worlds
Identical models, identical tasks, identical scoring rubric, real hardware and sim.
05
Correlate the two rankings
Compare the sim leaderboard against the real one. That correlation is the deliverable.

03  /  The metric

Two numbers do the judging.

MMRV — how far the policy ordering shifts between sim and real. Lower means the leaderboard barely moves.

Pearson r — how tightly sim scores track real scores. Higher means sim is reading the same signal.

Scoring is progress-based, not pairwise: an operator records the highest milestone each rollout reaches, so a policy that never succeeds still produces a usable signal.

Each milestone is written to be judged identically by a person in the real eval and by physics (contact, lift height, containment) in sim — the shared contract that keeps the correlation honest.
